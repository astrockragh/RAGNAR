Which PFS sky lines vary together

RAGNAR now groups the lines whose intensity ratios stay fixed over all the skies it simulates. These are the groups for the four PFS arms, tested on the sky spectra the LSFs were calibrated on.

What RAGNAR computes

Every spectrum of a RAGNAR run is one sky: here Trot 180 / 210 K, Tvib 8000 / 12000 K, hot population ×0.75 / 1 / 1.33, five chemistry draws, PWV 0 / 3 mm at ZD 30°, and three LSF widths (±5%), 360 skies per arm. For each pair of robust lines, Sij is the scatter of ln(Ii/Ij) over those skies, with I the line-only spectrum at the line centre (no centroider, no continuum). Sij = 0.01 means the ratio is fixed to 1%.

Lines are grouped by complete linkage at 1%, so every pair inside a group has Sij ≤ 1%. The four arms were run with the same grid and seed, so they simulate the same skies and lines can be grouped across arms. A group is a set of lines with a fixed ratio. Two thirds of the groups are one upper state (Λ-doublets, P/Q/R branches, and the same level in different bands); the rest join levels of one v′ whose rotational energies are close enough that Trot 180–210 K moves their ratio by less than 1%.

Against the PFS sky: b, r, n

The low-resolution data are four all-sky visits on two nights (2025-09-12 and 09-13), stacked per spectrograph. The Moon was 49° up and 72% full on night 1 and below the horizon on night 2, and the OH was about half as bright on night 2. For each line, di is the change of its ln amplitude from night 1 to night 2, averaged over the spectrographs. Lines that vary together keep their ratio, so di − dj = 0 within a group. The observed matrix is |di − dj|, on the same colour scale as Sij.

Amplitudes are measured the same way in the data and in RAGNAR's simulated spectra. The amplitude is the LSF Gaussian at the list wavelength, fitted within ±1σ, above a local baseline. The baseline is the mean of the faintest 30% of pixels between 2.5σ and 14σ on each side, joined by a straight line. So a smooth continuum in the data drops out, and RAGNAR's continuum is never used. The errors come from the scatter between the four spectrographs, which see the same sky (floor 1.0% in r and 3.5% in n per stack).

For flux calibration

  • Use lines from one RAGNAR group (covar_n ≥ 2) and measure them with a baseline taken from the data around each line. Require a peak of at least twice the baseline in every exposure. With that, groups held their ratios to 2% between two very different nights.
  • Lines with H₂O or O₂ absorption break groups even at high contrast. The fix4 grid only spans PWV 0–3 mm at one airmass. Drop flagged lines, or rerun the co-variation with a wider PWV and airmass grid.
  • The b groups are untested. On the moonlit night no b line in a group stood 2× above its baseline, and the blue ones (< 560 nm) changed by up to a factor of three. They need a dark night.
  • Groups that span arms measure the arms' relative response. Here r against n moved by between the nights.
  • The m data (six 60 s visits within 9 minutes) are noise-limited, and m was never observed with b, r or n. m groups and m-to-r groups are RAGNAR predictions for now.
