SEDM Sky-Line Calibration

SEDM is being reinstalled at LMU's 2.1 m telescope on the Wendelstein. Can it calibrate (a) its wavelength scale to 1 nm and (b) its relative spectral response on the night sky alone? We simulated the sky at R = λ/FWHM = 75, 100 and 150 over 340–1000 nm, and tested the flux calibration on mock observations per resolution.

Units: R is resolving power. Line brightness is in rayleigh (R) and spectra are in R nm⁻¹. PWV and zenith distances are for the Wendelstein.

Short answers

(a) Wavelength calibration to 1 nm

Yes, 557–890 nm8-4 band marginalNothing below 557 nm

Eight sky features from [O I] 557.7 nm to the OH 7-3 band hold their positions to σ ≤ 0.5 nm (most ≤ 0.2 nm). The 8-4 blend at 939 nm scatters by σ = 1.2 nm. A 10% error in the assumed resolution moves the OH blends by 0.2–0.7 nm. In 340–550 nm the brightest airglow feature reaches only R nm⁻¹, against about 10 R nm⁻¹ of dark-sky continuum.

(b) Relative flux calibration

Yes, 650–990 nm, to about 1%Needs a forward model

Each OH vibrational level emits several bands across the red, and at R = 100 those blends come 95–99% from one level. Fitting the whole 610–995 nm sky with one template per level recovered the instrument response, relative to 700 nm, to 0.1–0.5% with about 1,000 sky spaxels in 30 minutes, 0.2–1.1% with 100, and 0.5–3% with 10. The fit also recovers PWV (±0.6–0.9 mm). Measuring single band features and taking ratios is several to tens of times worse. PWV affects only the 8-4 band strongly.

(a) Wavelength calibration

Peak positions were measured as in the RAGNAR line lists, with a matched filter of the LSF width, over 1,080 simulated Wendelstein skies at R = 100. σλ is the scatter of each peak. The full range spans the extremes of every parameter at once. The last column gives the shift if the true resolution were 75 or 150.

  • The scatter meets the 1 nm budget everywhere except the 8-4 band. The 8-4 blend shape changes with Trot, moving its peak by up to 4 nm; use it with a low weight, or fit the sky model rather than the peak. The weak 6-1 blend and the 7-3 blend exceed 1 nm only at the extreme corners of the grid.
  • The resolution has to be known to roughly 10%. A wrong R changes the blend shapes. Fitting a model sky convolved with the LSF measured from arcs removes the problem.
  • Use the reference list made with the same peak finder. Raw peaks and matched-filter peaks of the same blend differ by up to 1.3 nm.
  • Below 557 nm the arcs must carry the solution. Light-pollution lines from the Munich area (not modelled) may help in the blue.

(b) Relative flux calibration

All flux measurements use the spectra without the centroider. The calibration target is the spectral shape of the response, normalised at 700 nm.

The groups that vary together

All lines from one upper vibrational level (v′) share that level's population, which carries all the night-to-night variation in vibrational excitation and chemistry. Between bands of the same level, only the rotational distribution matters: Trot and the hot population. Their ratios are set by the Einstein-A branching. Levels v′ = 6–9 each have two or three bands between 610 and 995 nm, and those carry the colour information.

PWV hits one band combination much harder than the others

The 8-4 band sits in the 940 nm water band, so the 8-3/8-4 ratio changes by about 1.5% per mm at low PWV, flattening as the strongest water lines saturate. The other same-level pairs change by 0.1–0.2% per mm. The 8-4 sky lines are also absorbed differently from a star's continuum: they fall partly on and partly between water lines. A response derived from 8-4 therefore needs a line-by-line telluric model, and cannot simply reuse a standard star's telluric correction.

Mock observations: how well the response comes back

Each mock is one random night: Trot 175–215 K, Tvib 7,500–12,500 K, hot population ×0.7–1.4, random chemistry for every species, PWV 0–27 mm, ZD 0–48°, continuum 5–25 R nm⁻¹ with a random slope, and an LSF width off by 3% (1σ) from the assumed one. Each is seen through a random smooth instrument response, sampled at 3 pixels per FWHM, with photon noise. We measured the response two ways:

  • Feature fits. The flux of each band blend from Gaussians at fixed positions plus a linear background. Ratios are compared with the ratio predicted for fiducial airglow.
  • Forward model. A fit of the whole 610–995 nm spectrum: one template per v′ level, plus O₂ b(0-1), [O I] 630/636 and Hα, each with a free amplitude. The templates are multiplied by a smooth response exp(s₁x + s₂x²). The fit includes the sky continuum seen through the same atmosphere, Trot, and optionally PWV, the hot-population fraction and Tvib.

Noise levels are given as detected photons per rayleigh of line flux. A rough estimate is 5 per spaxel in 30 minutes, if the IFU keeps the étendue per spaxel it had on the P60 and the total throughput is about 20%. 5,000 is then most of the ~1,000 sky spaxels of one exposure, 500 is about 100 spaxels, and 50 about 10.

  • Feature fits are not good enough. Even with the telluric absorption known, 8-3/8-4 comes back to ±3%. 7-2/7-3 fails (±30%), because 7-2 sits in the O₂ B band, where the sky continuum dips by about as much as the 7-2 emission.
  • The forward model gets to the 1% level, and needs the hot population. Fitting the hot-population fraction and Tvib cuts the noise-free error floor by about a factor of three. Without them, the error at most wavelengths tracks the true hot population almost perfectly (correlation ≥ 0.9).
  • The sky measures its own PWV. The fit recovers PWV to and Trot to at R = 100.
  • Resolution matters little. With 100 sky spaxels the response error at 940 nm is at R = 75 / 100 / 150. Individual band fluxes come back to (R = 100 / 150), because neighbouring v′ groups trade flux at low resolution. The response is less affected, because it comes from the shared level amplitudes.

What the mocks do not include

  • Errors in the Einstein-A branching ratios. The mocks use the same A values for the truth and the templates. Any error in the ratio between, say, the Δv = 4 and Δv = 5 bands enters the response directly. Noll et al. (2020) found systematic errors in all six published A sets they tested. One observation of a spectrophotometric standard, compared with a simultaneous sky fit, fixes these ratios for good. After that, the sky alone tracks the response.
  • Response structure finer than a quadratic. The sky only constrains the response at the band positions, roughly every 50 nm between 650 and 990 nm.
  • A real OH population has more freedom than the model's cold-plus-hot distribution. This includes high-J non-thermal populations and Λ-doublet asymmetries.
  • The Wendelstein atmosphere is approximated. We used Mauna Kea MODTRAN curves with the slant O₂, Rayleigh and H₂O columns matched. Ozone, aerosols and the effective extinction of a diffuse source are not modelled.

For reference: peaks from a single upper state need R ≳ 700

The original question asked for peaks that are ≥95–99% from one upper state (v′, J′, F). At R = 75–150 no OH peak gets more than about 45% from one upper state. Pairs at ≥95% purity, ≥100 nm apart and ≥5 R bright first appear at R ≈ 700, and at ≥99% at R ≈ 1500. 8-4 P1(3.5) never reaches 99%, because 8-4 Q1(6.5) and R2(11.5) lie within 0.035 nm of it.

What the simulation leaves out

  • Blue airglow. The O₂ Herzberg and Chamberlain bands are not in RAGNAR. They add faint, broad structure below about 450 nm, not clean features.
  • Light pollution and moonlight. Lamp lines could help the blue wavelength scale. Moonlight raises the continuum and adds solar absorption features; the forward model would need a solar template term.
  • Continuum shape. The mocks use a smooth power-law continuum. Real airglow continuum (NO₂, FeO) has broad structure near 600 nm, below the fitted range.

Method and files

RAGNAR (fast version), resolution mode, 334–1015 nm, Gaussian LSF at λ/FWHM = 75, 100, 150. RAGNAR grid: Trot 180/210 K, Tvib 8000/12000 K, hot population ×0.75/1/1.33, five chemistry draws, three LSF widths, Wendelstein PWV 2.7/10.6/20 mm and ZD 0/48° (Mauna Kea MODTRAN at PWV 2/8/15 mm, ZD 40/60°). Mocks use a vectorised copy of RAGNAR's line model, checked against it to 10⁻¹³, with the full Mauna Kea PWV 0–20 mm × ZD 40/50/60° grid. Line lists: lineLists/sedm_R*_{0,1}_wst_20261116. Scripts and tables: RAGNAR_new/sedm/ (run_sedm.py, analyze_sedm.py, mock_sedm.py).
