SEDM Sky-Line Calibration

Can the SEDM, at R = λ/FWHM ≈ 100 over 340–1000 nm, calibrate (a) its wavelength scale and (b) its relative flux scale on the night-sky emission lines? We simulated 1,080 sky spectra at that resolution, spanning the expected range of airglow and atmospheric conditions.

Units: R is resolving power. Line brightness is in rayleigh (R) and spectra are in R nm⁻¹.

Short answers

(a) Wavelength calibration

Yes, from 557 to 940 nmNothing below 557 nm

Eight bright features, plus a weak 6-1 blend, are found in every simulated spectrum. [O I] 557.7 nm is a near-perfect anchor (σ = 0.015 nm). The OH band blends hold their peak positions to σ = 0.1–0.4 nm (1–4% of a FWHM), except the 8-4 blend in the water band (σ = 1.1 nm). In 340–550 nm the brightest airglow feature reaches only R nm⁻¹, against roughly 10 R nm⁻¹ of dark-sky continuum.

(b) Relative flux calibration

Not with single upper states at R = 100Partly, 686–945 nm

Apart from [O I] 557.7 nm, no OH peak gets more than 46% of its flux from one upper state; each blends 5–8 of them. Same-upper-state pairs at ≥95% purity first appear at R ≈ 700, and at ≥99% at R ≈ 1500. A weaker version works at R = 100. The 8-3/8-4 and 7-2/7-3 band blends each come 97–99% from one vibrational level, and their ratios move by only ±1.0% and ±2.4% over the simulated airglow conditions. Using them needs a one-time calibration on standard stars and a line-by-line telluric correction.

The main driver is the parameter whose extremes move the peak most. The last column gives the shift if the true resolution were R = 75 or 150 instead of 100.

  • Use the same peak finder as the reference list. Raw-peak and matched-filter positions of the same blend differ by up to 1.3 nm (8-3: 728.86 vs 730.14 nm). Both lists are provided.
  • The LSF has to be known. A 10% error in R moves the OH blends by roughly 0.2–0.7 nm. Fitting a model sky convolved with the LSF measured from arcs avoids this problem, and is better than matching peaks.
  • The blue needs other references. No airglow feature below [O I] 557.7 nm is usable at R = 100. The arc lamps or light-pollution lines (not modelled) have to carry 340–557 nm.

(b) Relative flux calibration

All flux work below uses raw peak heights, without the centroider. Purity is the fraction of the observed peak at line centre that comes from a single upper state (v′, J′, F). The e/f Λ-doublet components share an upper level and count together. Purity is quoted for the worst case over the simulated airglow and atmosphere extremes.

Single-upper-state pairs need R ≳ 700

The example pair. At R = 100, 8-3 P1(3.5) (25 R) and 8-4 P1(3.5) (196 R) make up only 30% and 31% of their peaks. They reach 95% purity at R ≈ 630 and 570. 8-3 P1(3.5) reaches 99% at R ≈ 1000. 8-4 P1(3.5) stops at 98.9% at any resolution, because 8-4 Q1(6.5) and R2(11.5) lie within 0.005–0.035 nm of it.

Telluric absorption still matters for a pure pair. 8-4 P1(3.5) sits in the 940 nm water band. Its ratio to 8-3 P1(3.5) changes by 14.5% between PWV 1 mm at zenith and PWV 10 mm at ZD 50°. Its two Λ-doublet components are absorbed differently, by up to 25%. Using it needs a line-by-line transmission model for the night.

    Blends from the same vibrational level work at R = 100, with conditions

    At R = 100 the low-J lines of each OH band merge into one peak. Two bands from the same v′ then merge nearly the same upper levels with similar weights. Their ratio follows the v′ population and barely depends on Trot. This is the example pair in blended form. The table gives the half-range of each ratio over the simulated grid.

    • Only two pairs pass. 8-3/8-4 (±1.0–1.5%) and 7-2/7-3 (±2.4%, mostly from Trot). The 6-1 and 9-5 peaks are diluted by other bands and species. Of the peak pairs ≥100 nm apart, stay within ±3%, and all of these involve 8-3/8-4 or 7-2/7-3.
    • The absolute ratio is not known from physics alone. It depends on the Einstein-A branching between the Δv = 4 and Δv = 5 bands. Noll et al. (2020) found systematic errors in all six published A sets they tested. Calibrate each ratio once on spectrophotometric standards, then use it to follow changes in the response.
    • Correct the telluric absorption line by line. The 8-4 blend loses 9–11% between PWV 1 and 10 mm, while the lines see within 0.7–2.2% of the transmission a continuum source sees. The 7-2 lines fall on O₂ B-band lines and see 8% less transmission than a continuum source.
    • Coverage is 686–945 nm. Nothing ties the blue to the red. [O I] 557.7, Na D and [O I] 630 come from separate species, and their brightness relative to OH changes by tens of percent from night to night.
    • Spaxel-to-spaxel calibration needs none of this. Every spaxel of the IFU sees the same sky at the same time. The sky lines therefore calibrate the relative response between spaxels at each wavelength at any resolution.

    What the simulation leaves out

    • Blue airglow. The O₂ Herzberg and Chamberlain bands are not in RAGNAR. They add faint, broad structure below about 450 nm, not clean features.
    • Light pollution and moonlight. Mercury and sodium lamp lines at Palomar could help the blue wavelength scale. Moonlight raises the continuum and adds solar absorption features to the blends.
    • Site. Only Mauna Kea transmission curves exist, so PWV 1–10 mm stands in for Palomar's wetter sky. Palomar's lower altitude means deeper O₂ bands (relevant for 7-2) and more Rayleigh and aerosol extinction.
    • Diffuse emission. The airglow is given point-source transmission. For an extended source, scattering into the beam lowers the effective extinction, mostly in the blue. This matters when comparing sky-line fluxes with stars.
    • Continuum. RAGNAR's model continuum scales with the line width and is only right near σ = 0.1 nm, so it is not used here. Contrasts are quoted against a fixed 10 R nm⁻¹.

    Assumptions to confirm

    • Which SEDM and site: the P60 instrument at Palomar, or SEDM-KP at Kitt Peak? Site-specific transmission would firm up the 7-2 and 8-4 numbers.
    • Constant R = λ/FWHM = 100 with a Gaussian LSF, as the triple-prism design intends. A measured R(λ) from arcs would fix the blend positions.
    • The wavelength accuracy needed, in km s⁻¹.
    • Whether the flux goal is an absolute colour calibration from physics, or tracking the response after a standard-star calibration. Only the second is possible at R = 100.

    Method

    RAGNAR (fast version), resolution mode, 334–1015 nm, Gaussian LSF with λ/FWHM = 100 (and 75, 150 as brackets), no scattering wing. Grid: Trot 180/210 K, Tvib 8000/12000 K, hot-population fractions ×0.75/1/1.33, five chemistry draws, three LSF widths (±3.4%), PWV 1/5/10 mm, ZD 0/50°, background aerosols, Mauna Kea MODTRAN6. That gives 1,080 spectra per resolution. Wavelengths use the centroider lists; fluxes use raw peaks. Line lists: lineLists/sedm_R100_1_20261116 (centroider) and sedm_R100_0_20261116 (raw peaks). Scripts and tables: RAGNAR_new/sedm/.
